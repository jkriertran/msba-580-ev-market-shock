# Preliminary Washington Findings

## Recommended conclusion

Washington's ZEV-title share improved slightly after the February 2026 war, but
the increase is not statistically distinct from the post-incentive-rolloff
market and remains far below the pre-rolloff trajectory.

## Statewide evidence

- The pooled post-incentive-rolloff/pre-war share was 13.9% in November
  2025–February 2026.
- The March–June 2026 post-war share was 14.5%, an increase of 0.56 percentage
  points.
- Average Washington regular gasoline prices increased from $3.90 to $5.22 per
  gallon, or 33.8%.
- The pre-rolloff benchmark expected a 25.7% post-war share, 11.2 points above
  observed.
- The Newey–West combined post-incentive-rolloff coefficient is −11.59 points
  (p < 0.001).
- The additional post-war coefficient is +1.15 points (95% CI −0.97 to +3.28;
  p = 0.285).

The benchmark uses 102 training months. Among pre-specified candidates, the
quadratic-time-plus-season model has the lowest 42-month rolling RMSE at 3.66
percentage points, narrowly better than the linear model at 3.78.

## Gas-price association

- A prior-three-month average gas price lowers rolling RMSE from 3.66 to 3.37
  percentage points, a 7.9% improvement over the no-gas benchmark.
- The policy-adjusted association is +2.77 ZEV-share percentage points per
  $1/gallon (95% CI +0.27 to +5.27; p = 0.031).
- Adding Washington unemployment leaves the estimate nearly unchanged.
- Adding Washington electricity prices reduces it to +2.00 points (95% CI
  −0.52 to +4.52; p = 0.118).

Gasoline prices therefore add modest predictive information, but the estimate
is specification-sensitive and does not identify a causal fuel-price effect.

## Driving response

FHWA Washington VMT was 1.0% below the same month one year earlier in May 2026,
which is preliminary. VMT is analyzed as a separate response and is not used as
a control in the ZEV-title regression.

## County response

Thirty-two of 39 counties increased ZEV-title share, with a median change of
+3.34 points. Large-market changes included:

- Clark: +6.90 points
- Pierce: +4.70 points
- Snohomish: +4.51 points
- Kitsap: +4.12 points
- King: −3.18 points

County estimates exclude `Unknown or Out of State`. They describe heterogeneity
but are not independent replications of the statewide shock.

## Stated preferences

The conjoint analysis uses 123 completed ratings from 18 respondents. Price is
the clearest attribute block (p = 0.013). A $30,000 profile scores 1.30 points
above a $120,000 profile (p = 0.005). Brand and fuel economy cannot be cleanly
separated because Tesla was consistently paired with 110 MPGe.

## Managerial recommendations

1. Do not make a statewide inventory bet based on four post-war months.
2. Lead with affordability and total ownership cost.
3. Prioritize large county markets with positive observed movement.
4. Monitor later title releases for persistence and title-processing lag.
5. Do not describe the event coefficients as causal effects.

## Presentation wording

> Washington gasoline prices rose by roughly one-third after the war, while
> new light-duty ZEV-title share increased only 0.56 percentage points. The
> regression estimates a positive additional post-war change, but its interval
> includes zero. The current evidence supports monitoring and targeted county
> action. Lagged gasoline prices add modest predictive value, but the estimate
> weakens after electricity prices are included and does not establish that
> fuel pressure restored post-incentive-rolloff ZEV demand.
